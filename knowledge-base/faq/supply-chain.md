## Questions and answers

### How does ContexQ help us see beyond Tier 1 suppliers?

ContexQ connects direct suppliers to their wider relationships, including upstream companies, owners and locations where data is available. This can reveal dependencies that do not appear in a typical supplier master.

### Why does visibility into Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers matter?

Several direct suppliers can depend on the same upstream facility, component or material. A disruption at that shared point may affect multiple products, even when the Tier 1 supplier list looks diversified.

### Can we connect supplier risk to specific parts or products?

ContexQ starts from parts lists, purchasing records and supplier relationships. Linking those records can help teams see which components, lines or customer commitments may be exposed to a supplier issue.

### What types of supplier risks can be assessed?

Relevant categories can include financial distress, concentration, sanctions and trade restrictions, adverse media, ownership changes and climate or location-related disruption. Coverage depends on source availability and relevance.

### Can ContexQ help us find alternate suppliers?

Yes, the platform can help identify alternatives when supply is at risk. Potential options still need engineering, quality, commercial and qualification checks before being considered substitutes.

### Does ContexQ replace an ERP or procurement platform?

No. Purchasing and operational systems remain the systems of record. ContexQ is intended to add network context and external signals that can inform sourcing, supplier reviews and mitigation decisions.

### Can supply-chain findings support compliance and responsible sourcing?

Connected supplier and ownership records can support investigations into sanctions, sourcing concerns, labour practices and other obligations. The evidence helps compliance teams assess a situation; it does not establish compliance automatically.
