# Composite AI Scores

This is the ContexQ platform page for Composite AI Scores (menu label "Composite AI Scores"; browser page title "Composite AI Scores | ContexQ"). Page: /Platform/scores. ContexQ Composite AI Scores are explainable risk scores for customers, suppliers and counterparties, shaped by networks, history and peers, broken down into named components and mapped to clear next steps. Scores are delivered through "decision packs" for specific risks such as fraud and exposure, financial distress, sanctions proximity, ownership and control, location intelligence and shell detection.

## Risk rarely shows up in a company's own record.

Problems with conventional risk scoring:
- Scores look at the record, not the network: Owners, directors, suppliers and counterparties sit outside most risk scores, so exposure that runs through them goes unseen.
- Name checks miss how close the risk really is: A match on a list says little about its authority, severity or whether the link is direct or a few steps away.
- Black-box results stall decisions: A score without reasons is hard to challenge, approve or defend to an auditor or regulator.

## From scattered data to a decision you can defend.

How ContexQ Composite AI Scores work, in three steps:
1. Start from clean, resolved data: Your records and external data are mapped and resolved to the right people and companies. Uncertain mappings go to a person, never guessed.
2. Read the network around it: Owners, directors, suppliers, histories and peer groups are connected, so structural warning signs surface as the network is built.
3. Score, explain and act: Decision packs score the entity, break the result into named components and map it to a next step. Your team makes the call.

## Ready-made scores for specific decisions, tuned to your policies.

Each decision pack brings investigative expertise for a particular risk, combining the methods that suit that risk rather than a generic approach. The six ContexQ decision packs:
- Fraud and exposure: Combines unusual patterns in reported financials, exposure to sanctioned or high-risk counterparties through the network, and ownership that looks built to hide.
- Financial distress: Forecasts the likelihood of distress over a multi-year horizon and shows how the company compares with its peers.
- Sanctions proximity: Goes beyond name matching: weighs the authority of the list, the severity and grounds of the sanction, industry context, and whether the link is direct or through the network.
- Ownership and control: Follows ownership and control chains to the people who ultimately direct a company, including indirect forms of control.
- Location intelligence: Assesses the risk of where a company operates and whether it has real economic substance there.
- Shell detection: Flags companies whose structure and connections suggest little genuine activity, as soon as the network is built.

## Every score breaks down, and points to a next step.

Analysts see which components drove the result and where each input came from. Bands translate the score into handling your teams already understand.
- Named components: The evidence trail an analyst, committee or auditor needs.
- Recorded sources: Where every input came from, field by field.
- Peer context: How the entity compares with similar companies.

From band to handling (example for the financial distress pack):
- Low: Standard monitoring
- Elevated: Periodic review
- High: Enhanced due diligence
- Severe: Immediate exposure review
