## Spotting risk is only the beginning.

A warning is only useful if your team knows what caused it, who else is affected and what to do next. Checks that work in isolation often find the problem too late.

Three problems:
1. Warnings come late. Problems show up after payments have already slipped.
2. Clues are scattered. Ownership, payments and news sit in different places, so related risks look unrelated.
3. More work, not more clarity. Analysts still verify matches, trace links and decide what to look at first.

### Risk leaves clues before the first missed payment.

A timeline shows where ContexQ helps and where traditional checks start:
1. Pressure builds (where ContexQ helps): Income or employer stability weakens.
2. Habits change (where ContexQ helps): Cash gets tighter and payment patterns shift.
3. A payment is missed (where traditional checks start): Traditional checks finally notice.

Early signs a connected view can catch:
- Hidden pay-later borrowing: Short-term debt that normal affordability checks don't show.
- Strain at a shared employer: When one employer struggles, many customers can be affected.
- Smaller, more frequent top-ups: A sign that cash is getting tighter.
- Payments slipping slightly later: A small delay that often comes before a missed payment.

## From signal to action.

ContexQ does more than list risks. It connects the evidence, prioritises the warning and gives your team a clear place to start. How ContexQ turns signals into action, in five steps:

1. Bring data together: Internal and external records in one view.
2. Recognise who is who: The same person or business across all records.
3. Connect the dots: Owners, partners and shared dependencies.
4. Spot what's changing: Signals scored and exposure assessed.
5. Know what to do first: Cases ranked, with evidence attached.

### A warning should lead to a decision, not another list.

Every alert helps your team:
- Understand it: see what changed, who is connected and the evidence behind the flag.
- Prioritise it: the most relevant, confirmed risks rise to the top of the queue.
- Act in time: investigate, escalate, monitor or step in before the loss grows.

Example alert card (labelled "Example"; Alert · High priority):
- What happened: Payments from several customers who work for the same employer are slipping later each month.
- Why it matters: The employer shows signs of strain, and the customers connected to it could be affected next.
- Evidence: Payment records; Employer filing; Shared lender ("Evidence attached").
- What to do next: Review the connected customers and decide whether to contact, monitor or escalate.

### A fuller picture of every risk.

Keep your existing checks. We add the connections they miss. Four areas around one customer:

- The customer
  - Normal checks: Product, channel, industry, location and screening alerts.
  - ContexQ adds: Links to risky industries, restricted directors or unusual shared addresses.
- The group behind them
  - Normal checks: Declared ownership layers and high-risk countries.
  - ContexQ adds: Ties to closed companies, ownership that circles back and missing filings.
- Their partners
  - Normal checks: Direct payment alerts, watchlists and bad news about partners.
  - ContexQ adds: Hidden owners or disqualified directors behind the businesses they trade with.
- Their financial behaviour
  - Normal checks: Expected transaction volumes, cash ratios and high-risk payment routes.
  - ContexQ adds: Payments that don't match filings, activity that differs from similar businesses, and money moving in circles within a group.

## What can your team do after a risk is flagged?

Understand the risk, decide what to review first and explain the decision. Four ways a team can use ContexQ for risk management (shown as tabs):

### 1. Full customer view: One profile. The evidence around it.

See everything connected to a customer in one place.
- Ownership and links: Who owns them, who they are linked to and shared addresses.
- Financial and peer signals: Strain, debt pressure and how they compare to similar customers.
- Payments and public records: Transactions and records that support or challenge the picture.

Example, a customer profile (Example customer, profile with connected evidence): flags "Suspended from trading", "Offshore tax haven link", "Accounting anomaly" and "Intercompany billing pattern". What the connection reveals: The owner is linked to a company in liquidation, which also shares a lender with several of your customers.

### 2. Unusual trade and industry patterns: Are these trading partners a credible match?

Compare real trading relationships to flag unusual buyer-seller pairs, even when industry codes are missing or unreliable.
- Infer business activity: Use company records and trade history.
- Find unusual trade pairs: Learn normal patterns and flag rare buyer-seller pairs.
- Rank by confidence and impact: Rank each outlier by confidence and business share.

Importer-Exporter Industry Pair Matrix (Example):
- Agri Wholesale → Food Producer: Expected (847 trades)
- Textiles → Apparel Retail: Expected (239 trades)
- IT Wholesale → Crop Farming: Rare Outlier (2 trades)

Why network context beats static keyword rules: A few trades between mismatched industries may deserve more attention than hundreds of routine keyword alerts.

### 3. High-risk case reviews: Give analysts the evidence, not another data hunt.

Start each case with ranked, cross-checked evidence.
- Triage screening alerts: Check each alert against the customer and connected network.
- Verify true ownership: Verify beneficial owners and related-party exposure against official records.
- Build the case summary: Bring payment history and counterparties into one evidence-backed summary.

Analyst Investigation Workbench:
- Alert triage: Pre-checked in network
- Ownership chart: Registry verified
- Year-over-year flows: Consolidated
- Case narrative: Auto-drafted with citations

Consistent decisions driven by the customer's true risk: Every analyst starts with the same ownership links, transaction history, and ranked evidence, which makes reviews more consistent.

### 4. News monitoring: Turn news into facts your team can verify.

Extract people, companies, relationships and allegations, rather than relying only on name matches.
- Global & local news at scale: Combine licensed news, public reports, and internal intelligence in supported languages.
- Extract facts and relationships, not just names: Link each entity, relationship, and allegation to its source sentence.
- Evidence-weighted matching into the network: Cross-check findings against customer and payment records; prioritise corroborated matches.

Private, Source-Cited Media Intelligence:
- Deployment: 100% inside your perimeter
- Data privacy: Zero external vendor leakage
- Audit trail: Exact text span cited

Explainable to model governance and internal audit: Show the source sentence and the shared address, director, or other link connecting the article to your portfolio.

## Every check helps. Only the connected view sees the pattern.

Teams build up their defences in stages. Each one catches more, but the first four still judge one customer at a time. ContexQ adds the fifth, on top of what you already run.

How risk defences build up, and where ContexQ fits:
1. Credit scores and limits (one customer at a time): Show a customer's history, not what is changing now.
2. Affordability checks (one customer at a time): Miss pay-later debt and strain at a shared employer.
3. Watchlists and screening (one customer at a time): Only catch names that are already flagged.
4. AI risk scoring (one customer at a time): Ranks the same single-customer signals better.
5. The connected view (ContexQ adds the connections): Links customers by employer, owner, lender and payment flow: the pattern, not the record.
