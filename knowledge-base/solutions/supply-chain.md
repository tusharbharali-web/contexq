## Procurement fraud: Uncover procurement fraud before it costs you.

Procurement fraud hides in relationships and buying patterns that look normal one invoice at a time. ContexQ connects vendor, employee, ownership and purchasing records to bring them to light. Eight procurement fraud risks ContexQ helps uncover:

1. Conflict of interest: An employee is quietly connected to a vendor. Vendor and employee records are compared for shared bank accounts, addresses and phone numbers.
2. Ghost vendors: A vendor with no real business behind it. Company records reveal home addresses, no trading history or a newly formed company.
3. Bid rigging: Competing bidders that aren't really competing. Bidders owned by the same parent, or sharing directors or addresses, are linked.
4. Favouritism: One vendor keeps winning without competition. Repeated single-bid awards and unbroken invoice sequences stand out.
5. Split purchases: Orders broken up to avoid approval. Orders clustering just below a limit, from the same requester and vendor, are flagged.
6. Buying after the fact: Purchases approved after the work is done. Requests dated after the invoice are found, across every document, not a sample.
7. Payment diversion: Bank details changed just before a payment run. New details are checked against employee records and other vendors before money leaves.
8. Sanctions and reputation: Paying a sanctioned or high-risk owner without knowing it. Ownership is traced to the real owner and screened against sanctions lists and adverse news.

### How two of these come to light

- Conflict of interest: A vendor and an employee turn out to share a bank account and an address. On their own, both records look normal.
- Split purchases: Orders pile up just below the approval limit. Legitimate framework orders are set aside first, so only the unusual ones remain.

With ContexQ, every document is checked, not a sample. Each finding arrives as a clear case: the vendor, the purchases, who approved them and when, with a suggested next step.

Fair and careful: A pattern is a reason to look, not proof. Documents are reviewed before anyone is named, and holding a payment is always a person's decision.

## From insight to action: Every finding comes with a recommended next step.

ContexQ prepares the evidence and the options, in the tools your teams already use. The person accountable makes the call. Three examples:

1. Disruption: A storm is heading for a port several of your suppliers use. Already identified: the exposed sites, materials and shipments, and approved alternatives for some parts. Recommended: Re-route the shipments at risk and pre-approve an alternative for the most exposed parts. Decided by your head of supply chain.
2. Supplier selection: A new candidate looks strong, but its only site is in a flood-risk area. It also shares a parent company with one of your existing suppliers. Recommended: Keep it on the shortlist, with a site-risk condition that follows it into onboarding. Decided by your category manager.
3. Procurement integrity: A vendor's bank details changed just before a payment run. The new account also appears on an employee record. Recommended: Hold the payment and verify the change by calling the vendor on a known number. Decided by your accounts payable reviewer.
