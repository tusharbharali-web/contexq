## Turn KYC from a cost into an advantage.

Large banks spend heavily to keep customer files current, yet the picture is often out of date by the time a review is signed off. ContexQ changes where that effort goes.

Statistics shown on the KYC page (labelled "Industry figures cited in ContexQ research."):
- 6+ months to refresh a single large corporate customer.
- 3-5 years between scheduled reviews, while risk keeps moving.
- 54% of KYC professionals want broader data on people and companies.

Four benefits of ContexQ for KYC:
- Less effort per review: Customer records, owners and outside sources arrive together, so analysts spend their time judging rather than searching.
- Reviews when risk changes: Reviews are triggered by real events instead of the calendar, so customers aren't asked for the same documents again for nothing.
- Risk seen sooner: Changes at owners, directors and parent companies are picked up when they happen, not years later.
- New business from the same data: The group companies, owners and partners found for KYC also show where your bank could grow the relationship.

## See the risk around the customer, not just the customer.

A customer's own profile rarely tells the whole story. Add ownership, trading partners and behaviour to your existing rating model, so reviews reflect the risks around the customer too.

Your rating model and policies stay. ContexQ adds what sits around the customer, and shows the evidence behind every change in rating.

The four layers ContexQ looks at in KYC:
1. The customer: Profile and screening results, with related parties and directors added.
2. The group behind them: Layered or looping ownership, missing shareholder details and links to former customers.
3. Their partners: Who the customer deals with, including sanctions, politically exposed person (PEP) and negative-news signals.
4. Their behaviour: Activity compared with what you expected at onboarding, including shifting payment patterns and money moving within a corporate group.

## Connections that change the decision.

Four situations ContexQ brings to light in KYC, and the next step your team can take:

1. Returning customer: A new applicant's director once ran a company you exited (possible match). Next step: Pause onboarding until the match is confirmed and the exit reason reviewed.
2. Ongoing monitoring: A new director at the customer's offshore parent may be a politically exposed person (appointed this week; possible PEP). Next step: Bring the review forward and confirm the match before re-rating.
3. Deeper review: Ownership loops back to the customer, and some owners are unknown (a holding company owns the customer again). Next step: Ask the customer for the missing owners before the review is closed.
4. Growth: Two companies in a customer's group bank elsewhere (sister companies under the parent). Next step: Share the group view with the relationship manager for an introduction.

"Possible matches are always confirmed by your team before any decision."

## Review the changes that matter, when they happen.

New directors, ownership changes and negative news can change the picture between scheduled reviews. ContexQ checks each update for relevance, so your team follows up on what matters and always starts from current information.

Three steps ContexQ follows for each update:
1. Connect the change: Match each update to the right customer and the companies around them.
2. Judge its relevance: Check whether it affects risk or meets a review rule you have set.
3. Hand it to your team: Send the alert and its supporting context to your existing workflow.

For example: a change in address formatting simply updates the record. A new director at the parent who may be a PEP goes to an analyst to confirm.

Caveat: Updates depend on the sources available, and they work alongside the periodic reviews you are required to run.
