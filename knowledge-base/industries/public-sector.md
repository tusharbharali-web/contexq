## Important decisions. Incomplete connections.

An agency may hold the information it needs across several systems. Inconsistent identities and disconnected records make it harder to see which relationships matter.

- **Less time reconciling records**: Help teams recognise the same person or organisation across different sources.
- **More context around a review**: Bring relevant ownership, directorship and payment relationships into view.
- **A clearer next step**: Focus specialist attention on the questions and supporting records that need examination.

## Support the decisions behind your agency's mission.

Explore a use case to see the information it brings together and the outcome it can support. The public-sector page lists four use cases:

### Public procurement — Understand who you are doing business with.
- Bring supplier records, corporate ownership and contract history together to help teams examine potential conflicts and relationships between bidders.
- Outcome: A more informed basis for supplier due diligence.
- Question: Was the relationship between bidders disclosed? Compare directorship and ownership records with supplier declarations and tender requirements.

### Tax & customs — Look beyond the individual declaration.
- Connect business identities, ownership and relevant trade information to help analysts examine inconsistencies and relationships across records.
- Outcome: A clearer starting point for risk assessment and further enquiries.
- Question: Do these records describe consistent business activity? Examine a declaration alongside the business identity, related organisations and relevant trade records.

### Grants & payments — See the connections across applications.
- Bring recipient, organisation and payment records together to help programme teams examine possible duplicate applications and connected recipients.
- Outcome: More focused checks on the allocation of public funds.
- Question: Are these separate entitlements or a possible duplicate? Link applications to the relevant recipient, then examine the programme rules and payment history.

### Investigations — Follow the relationships that matter.
- Connect people, organisations and relevant records so investigators can explore a case in context and identify the questions that need further evidence.
- Outcome: Less fragmented case preparation and a more informed direction for investigation.
- Question: Which relationship deserves the next enquiry? Explore the people and businesses around a case and identify where supporting evidence is needed.
