## See the group behind separate applications.

A shared detail becomes useful when teams can examine who is connected, how they are connected and what happened. This interactive example is aimed at fraud & investigation teams and has three steps:

1. **Separate records — Begin with the application in front of you.** Each subscriber record can appear ordinary on its own. Explore the connections to see what a wider review could include. Evidence: Original application details; Available device and activation records; Dealer and channel information.
2. **Reveal connections — A wider group comes into view.** All three applications share a dealer. Two also share an observed device. These are different relationships, giving the team a defined group to examine. Evidence: Dealer relationship across applications; Additional device link between two records; Distinct applicants retained.
3. **Review evidence — Review the evidence before deciding.** Examine the underlying records and timing together. Establish whether the connections have an ordinary explanation or need further investigation. Evidence: Source records supporting each connection; Activation timing and available activity; Reason for review and legitimate explanations.

A shared device or dealer is a clue to assess, not proof of fraud.
