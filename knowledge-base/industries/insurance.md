## An isolated record rarely tells the whole story.

A customer, policy or claim can look ordinary on its own. The wider picture is often spread across systems, teams and lines of cover - and it's what sits between those records that matters.

1. **Related claims are easy to miss**: The same person, contact detail or service provider may appear in different cases.
2. **Records sit in different places**: Claims, policy and customer information may be held by separate teams and systems.
3. **Investigators have to join the dots**: Time is spent searching across records before a team can understand the wider situation.
4. **Decisions need more than one record**: Without context, it can be harder to decide which cases need a closer review.

It is not just about what is in a claim. It is about what else is connected to it. That wider context can give an investigator a better place to start.

## Six moments in the life of a policy

Pick a moment to see what goes wrong today, and what changes when every record points to the real people involved. Visitors can highlight the moments for their role ("Show what matters if you look after"): Everything; Claims and fraud (moments 4, 5, 6); Underwriting (moments 2, 3); Compliance (moments 3, 4); Customers (moments 1, 4); Risk and finance (moments 2, 5, 6).

**1. Getting a quote**
- What goes wrong today: A loyal customer or their family asks for a quote and looks like a stranger, so they get a generic price and no recognition.
- What ContexQ connects: Their existing policies, their household, and their past claims, even when the details are spelled differently.
- What changes: You recognise families and long-standing customers from the first click.

**2. Checking the risk**
- What goes wrong today: A business hides who really owns it, or a car is kept at a different address from the one on the form.
- What ContexQ connects: Company records, property and weather-risk data, and vehicle records.
- What changes: You price the real risk, not just the one written on the form.

**3. Starting the policy**
- What goes wrong today: Cover is issued to someone on a sanctions list, or a life policy is used to move dirty money.
- What ContexQ connects: Sanctions lists, records of people in public office, and news coverage.
- What changes: Every new policyholder is checked before cover begins.

**4. Making a claim**
- What goes wrong today: Honest claims wait in the same queue as suspicious ones, and repeat claimants slip through under slightly different names.
- What ContexQ connects: Past claims, name variations, and the people and places that keep showing up together.
- What changes: Genuine claims are paid quickly, and only the suspicious ones go for a closer look.

**5. Paying out**
- What goes wrong today: The same repair garage, clinic, or law firm appears claim after claim, quietly pushing up costs.
- What ContexQ connects: Every repairer, clinic, and lawyer across all of your claims and lines of cover.
- What changes: You spot providers who appear too often, and pay fair costs.

**6. Recovering costs**
- What goes wrong today: Another party was at fault, but nobody connected the dots in time to recover the money.
- What ContexQ connects: Vehicles, companies, and other insurers' policyholders involved in the same event.
- What changes: You find who else should pay, with the evidence to back it up.

## Four claims. One name, spelled four ways.

They arrive weeks apart, for different kinds of cover, and land with different handlers. On their own, none of them looks unusual. This is an illustrative example. Shared details are a reason to review, not proof of wrongdoing.

- Car accident — Reported 14 March · Handler A — J. Patel — Rear-end collision, whiplash
- Water damage at home — Reported 22 March · Handler B — Jay Patel — Burst pipe, kitchen repairs
- Second car accident — Reported 8 April · Handler C — Jaiveer P. — Side collision, car written off
- Back injury at work — Reported 19 April · Handler A — J.V. Patel — Lifting injury, time off work

When the visitor selects "Connect the claims": Once the names line up, the same people and details appear across the claims: Same doctor, Same lawyer, Same repair garage, Same witness.

### Why these claims were flagged
- Four spellings of one name share the same phone number and address.
- The same doctor, lawyer, repair garage and witness appear across the claims.
- The claims came in close together, through different teams, so nobody saw the pattern.
- The related records are presented together for an investigator to review, with each link visible.

## How ContexQ works: resolve, infer, explain.

Three steps used across every insurance scenario - the same method behind the four-claim example above.

1. **Resolve — Who is this, really?** Match name variations, contact details and identifiers across your records to recognise the same person or business, while keeping genuinely different ones apart.
2. **Infer — How are they connected?** Surface the relationships between policyholders, claimants, service providers and payees - across claims, policies and lines of cover.
3. **Explain — Why does it matter?** Present the connections, the records that support them and the reason for attention - so a person makes the final decision, with the evidence visible.

## Three areas where the connected view pays back.

Across the policy lifecycle, the same connected information supports distinct kinds of decision.

- **Claims and investigations**: Surface relationships across claims, claimants, service providers and payment recipients - so investigators start with the wider picture already assembled.
- **Underwriting context**: Bring an applicant's existing policies, prior claims history and related parties into view at the point of quote - so the risk is priced on what is known, not only on what is declared.
- **Customer and household view**: Understand how policies and customer records relate across a household or small business - while keeping distinct people distinct - so renewals, cross-sell and service decisions see the whole relationship.

The examples above describe potential applications. Fit depends on the data available, the use case and how each insurer operates. We are happy to discuss which apply to your environment.
